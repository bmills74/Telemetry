# Features to implement

## Shorter spoken racing-line feedback

**Status:** Planned

When a driver asks about their line through a corner, give a brief answer they can use while driving. Lead with the most useful observation and keep the advice to one adjustment.

- Drop the spoken evidence list of entry, apex, exit and maximum offsets. Keep detailed measurements available for post-drive review.
- Use plain language and round distances when they help. For example, say “You turned in slightly early” when turn-in timing supports that conclusion, or “You were about a metre wide at the apex” when position data supports it.
- Treat small deviations as close to the reference. For an exit offset of roughly 0.2 m, say “Good exit” instead of reading out the measurement.
- Mention the largest deviation only when it adds useful information. Say where it happened and in which direction, such as “You were about two metres wide on entry.”
- Use steering and timing data to judge turn-in. A lateral offset alone can show whether the car was wide or tight at a point, but cannot establish that turn-in was early or late.

Example spoken answer: “You turned in slightly early and ran about a metre wide at the apex. Good exit. Try turning in a touch later.”

Use the voice-drive harness to review the wording against recorded corners. Check that small offsets get a qualitative answer, meaningful deviations have a location and direction, and missing measurements do not produce a driving diagnosis.
